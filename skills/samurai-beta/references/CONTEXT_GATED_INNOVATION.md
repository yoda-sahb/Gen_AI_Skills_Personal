# Context-gated strategy and innovation diagnostics — Samurai v2.5-beta

**Experimental module, not a change to Samurai's seven stages.** Apply after the Stage 2 root hypotheses and Mandatory Boundary Challenge, and revisit at Stage 5 when comparing mechanisms. The objective is to detect missed resource-allocation or learning-capability constraints, not force every problem into disruption theory or an organizational audit.

## Routing: use only what the case earns

- **Case A — Disruption and allocation:** Trigger only when a proposed solution addresses an emerging market, new nonconsumer, substantially different business model, or an opportunity that appears disadvantaged under the incumbent's established profit/customer criteria. See Module A.
- **Case B — Innovation engine audit:** Trigger when the question concerns repeated discovery, product portfolio growth, survival of learning across projects, systemic incentive conflicts, or a proposed new innovation organization. See Module B.
- **Neither trigger:** For ordinary defect repair, correct contract settlement, a local data-pipeline issue with an accountable owner, or otherwise adequate existing methods, return \`NOT_TRIGGERED\` and continue to the smallest useful action.
- **Both trigger:** Run a short diagnostic of each independently, then relate the results to the same decision. Do not duplicate evidence or invent an enterprise transformation.
- Missing company-internal evidence means \`ASSESSMENT_UNRESOLVED\`, never an invented numeric score or prescriptive reorganization.

The original Boundary Challenge remains mandatory whenever its triggering conditions apply. These modules **cannot clear** unresolved rights, external actors, obligations, decision ownership, or contradictory baseline evidence.

## Module A — Christensen-inspired disruption and resource-allocation challenge

Clayton Christensen's *The Innovator's Dilemma* (1997) distinguishes sustaining innovation from cases where entrants gain ground through lower-end or new-market opportunities and then improve. It does **not** imply that all technological change is disruptive or that established companies are incapable of innovation. This is an independent operational diagnostic, not an official Christensen skill.

Ask, in this order:

1. **Customer/job:** Which current customer values the incumbent's existing performance? Which overlooked, lower-end, or currently nonconsuming group would value a different performance profile?
2. **Entry condition:** What makes the proposed offering accessible or attractive to that group when mainstream incumbents do not serve it adequately?
3. **Economic asymmetry:** Are initial margins, scale, channels, metrics, support costs or growth expectations systematically unattractive to the incumbent?
4. **Resource allocation:** Which actual planning, budget, talent, priority or product-governance decision would rationally favor the established business? What source documents or interviews support this, rather than an imagined culture?
5. **Improvement trajectory:** Is there a plausible path for the new offering to become good enough for broader markets? What would falsify that trajectory?
6. **Rival explanations:** Could this simply be a sustaining feature, new category, market extension, or efficient use of existing teams?
7. **Organization test:** Does evidence show a different business requires protected resources/criteria? If not, do not propose a spinoff or separate innovation organization.

**Outputs:**
\`SUSTAINING | POSSIBLE_NEW_MARKET | POSSIBLE_LOW_END | OTHER_INNOVATION | UNRESOLVED | NOT_TRIGGERED\`, with the customer, incumbent metric, allocation evidence, challenger case and kill test.

Never use "AI," "disruption," "digital," or "big incumbent" alone as trigger or proof.

## Module B — Innovation Engine Audit routing

The relevant innovation-assessment method asks whether a company retains learning and can repeatedly turn uncertainty into useful new capabilities. Its use is diagnostic, not a substitute for Stage 2 causal work or Stage 7 delivery evidence.

Use the smallest evidence-supported subset of these dimensions:
- **Learning continuity:** Are findings reusable and do they survive transfer, staff changes and reorganization?
- **Truth flow:** Can negative evidence reach decision-makers and change the proposed problem or product?
- **Exploration capacity:** Can teams test multiple hypotheses or are they measured only on delivering a preselected solution?
- **Enterprise versus local incentives:** Do incentives reward an end-to-end outcome or only team-level throughput/territory?
- **Systems leverage:** Are reusable interfaces, decision evidence and accumulated capabilities left behind?
- **Governance and patience:** Who owns validation, funding gates, accepting failure, and graduating proven results?
- **Complementary talent:** Can technical, domain, consumer and commercial experts contest one another's assumptions productively?

Distinguish **planting** (learning/new options) from **harvesting** (improving/monetizing established capabilities). Both are valid; the failure hypothesis is harvesting labeled as novel invention while exploration or reuse deteriorates.

**Evidence rule:** When publicly researching another company, use \`OBSERVED | DOCUMENTED | REPORTED | INFERRED | UNKNOWN\` per dimension and record counterevidence. Without direct internal evidence, **do not generate a 1–5 or /100 organization score**. A specific observed handoff failure may justify one local action without demonstrating that the entire innovation engine is broken.

**Outputs:** \`ENGINE_CAPABILITY_SUPPORTED | ENGINE_GAP_HYPOTHESIS | ASSESSMENT_UNRESOLVED | NOT_TRIGGERED\`, named failure mechanism, disconfirming evidence, minimum test and affected owner.

## Connection to Samurai and adjacent skills

- Stage 2 still identifies competing root causes and the precise unit of analysis.
- Boundary Challenge still decides whether an architecture is premature.
- This reference challenges **business-model/resource allocation** and **repeatability/organizational learning** only when triggered.
- Stage 5 compares local repair, existing-team integration, partner solutions and genuinely new mechanisms. Modules must let current capabilities shrink the proposal.
- Stage 6 retains the patent and novel-transfer gates unchanged.
- Stage 7 requires real production/adoption proof and named receiving owners.
- A buyer-specific premium analysis belongs to the separate Product Value-Strategy skill; do not import its full framework as another mandatory Samurai stage.
- A dual-outcome innovation frontier test belongs to a distinct specialized method when two protected outcomes seem incompatible.

## Case guards

**Creator compensation:** A merchant's commission discrepancy does not become a disruption case merely because a competitor could recruit creators. Start with entitlements, provider reconciliation and disputes. \`A=NOT_TRIGGERED\`. Run \`B\` only if documented repeated learning loss or systemic ownership failure appears. No new AI system or spinoff by default.

**Fictional appliance company's emerging digital service:** Evaluate whether an underserved customer/job and different unit economics exist before applying the disruption label. A creator app may be a sustaining extension. \`A=UNRESOLVED\` until market entry/trajectory evidence is provided. If multiple teams independently recreate the same data contract and cannot share results, \`B=ENGINE_GAP_HYPOTHESIS\` pending corroboration. Do not invent headcount, budgets or actual company defects.

**Local batch tracking bug:** A verified deterministic job drops qualified sales; patch/test it and settle owed amounts. Both modules \`NOT_TRIGGERED\`. Do not slow correction with a full strategic assessment.

**Negative control:** A company can have high AI spending, a well-known innovation team and many launches but weak proof of accumulated learning. Conversely, separate teams do not automatically imply a broken innovation engine.

## Evaluation and release rules

For future withheld-case testing, compare v2.4-beta and v2.5-beta on: missed disruption/allocation mechanisms, false-positive disruption classification, unsupported organization scores, needless architecture, time/verbosity overhead, and preservation of creator contract/payment and boundary protections. Include cases where **neither** module applies and where the proposed "disruption" is sustaining.

The initial SharkNinja application is a **known-case editorial regression**, not blind validation or evidence that v2.5 improves performance. No synthetic fixture should be represented as an independently replicated model benchmark.
