# Samurai missions — staged fictional cases

All five cases below are fictional teaching instruments. They do not describe an actual company's data or capabilities. Facilitators should reveal one round at a time, only **after** the trainee commits an answer in writing.

## Mission 1 — The Attribution Trap

**Initial brief:** A consumer-products brand wants to unify retailer, influencer, ecommerce, device, and support datasets to solve attribution. The executive frames it as a multi-tenant data-wrangling issue.

**Round A — Stakeholders:** Each source has different schemas and keys. Marketing, Retail, Data, Service, and Digital each own a different slice. Trainee distinguishes messy/fragmented truth from fundamentally unobserved truth.

**Round B — Hidden reality:** A consumer hears about a product via a private family conversation after a creator's video; purchase happens at a physical retailer. Neither conversation nor a reliable purchase-identity link enters the manufacturer's systems. Ask: can any ETL recover that exact path? **No.**

**Round C — Existing capabilities:** A mature identity/customer-profile system and conversational service capabilities already exist. New customer CDP proposals should shrink or stop.

**Round D — Permission constraint:** Customers will not sign up solely for attribution, and some refuse data collection. Test genuine utility, purpose-limited permission, opt-in bias, and privacy-preserving alternatives.

**Round E — Measurement constraint:** The brand needs incremental marketing impact, not only richer context. Ask how randomized holdouts/aggregate retail sales could estimate lift without reconstructing private paths, and what happens when measurable outcomes are unavailable.

**Round F — Incentives:** Vertical teams retain product ownership, Legal owns permission policy, Data operates customer infrastructure, and Commercial owns media budgets. Define mission decision rights and graduation.

**Graduation evidence:** rejects false perfect attribution, reuses existing substrate, separates individual context from aggregate causal lift, defines falsifiable customer value and a path to use.

## Mission 2 — Post-purchase friction

**Initial brief:** Reduce service costs 20% via a support chatbot.

**Reveal:** The majority of contacts involve setup, feature discovery or unclear product education, repeated across categories rather than defects. A generic chatbot duplicates existing capabilities.

**Task:** move from reducing support-contact count to improving customer product success, with a cross-product state/action/outcome loop and protected satisfaction/effort metrics.

## Mission 3 — Three assistants

**Initial brief:** Three product divisions want AI assistants. Each has budget and specialized knowledge.

**Reveal:** The company has a substantial shared identity and agent substrate. Division-specific requirements are legitimately different.

**Task:** design the thinnest shared capability, with domain-owned experiences and consent-scoped shared learning, not three stacks or an overcentralized monolith.

## Mission 4 — Promotions optimizer

**Initial brief:** Improve promotional ROAS independently across retailers and markets.

**Reveal:** Local optimization creates supply imbalance, channel conflict, customer disappointment, and support cost elsewhere.

**Task:** find an enterprise outcome and constraints that local ROAS misses, define a decision contract and test incentives and stakeholder ownership. Do not assume every local metric is wrong.

## Mission 5 — Invisible boundary

**Initial brief:** A business has connected products, CRM, service, and ecommerce. Leadership asks for better integration.

**Reveal:** A large customer population uses the product without registration; no user-level profile exists. Introducing surveillance would fail the customer's value/privacy test.

**Task:** produce at least three choices: accept uncertainty, run aggregate studies, or introduce voluntary digital value exchanges. Select a minimum design and a decisive test.

## Independent battery integrity

These worked consumer-products cases are *practice*. For spontaneous novel-transfer certification, pick a previously unseen domain (e.g. industrial maintenance, education, logistics) and do not provide analogies or source structures in advance.
