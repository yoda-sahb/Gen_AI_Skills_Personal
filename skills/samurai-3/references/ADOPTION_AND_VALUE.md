# Relative value and adoption

## Value comparison

For a real decision, identify the chooser, desired outcome, time horizon, actual alternatives, value drivers and their importance. Classify performance versus the best viable alternative as advantage, parity, disadvantage or unknown. Do not treat relevance as proof of advantage.

Check what budget and resource owners will actually do. A saved hour may be reassigned rather than eliminated; a more effective advertisement may cause reallocation rather than reduce spending. Identify the product boundary, value capture and non-priorities.

This lens draws on the public Product Value-Strategy skill and its described Dwight Porter lineage. Later behavior checks are an integrated extension, not automatically original Porter doctrine.

## Adoption

Use high-level principles associated with Chip and Dan Heath's Switch: specify the rational next move, establish a legitimate human motive, and remove environmental friction. Seek bright spots and make the first step small. Do not use change techniques to disguise missing proof or to bypass consent.

Track real trial, repeated use, task success, user experience and ownership. A presentation, training attendance and a high satisfaction score are not production adoption. If adoption is weak, investigate whether the underlying value proposition is wrong before launching a persuasion campaign.
