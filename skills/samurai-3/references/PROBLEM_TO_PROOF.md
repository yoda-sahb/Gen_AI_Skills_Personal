# Proof, value and dual mandates

Samurai 3.0 has a simple user-facing sequence: Unlock, Root, Reframe, Prove, Build to One, Graduate. The full seven-stage Samurai chain remains the deeper engine. These are complementary views, not identical source doctrines.

## Relative value

Specify the decision-maker, desired outcome, strongest real alternative including reuse or no action, buyer-value drivers, evidence strength, comparative performance, and how the choice creates value. Relative value is not the same as technical novelty. Ask what an actor will actually do with any budget or time supposedly saved.

## Dual mandate

Only activate when two legitimate outcomes conflict and neither extreme is acceptable. Name both extremes, identify a true outcome invariant, consider the protected customer, describe the current causal tradeoff, state a solution-independent frontier-shift condition, consider multiple competing mechanisms including infeasibility, isolate the load-bearing assumption, run a cheap kill test, then design architecture only if a mechanism survives. Wide solution freedom does not permit violating law, safety, privacy, physics, or customer trust.

## Proof ladder

Concept, storyboard, synthetic interactive demonstration, working prototype, internal trial, external beta, controlled evidence of outcome, production, and observed adoption are separate evidence levels. Always label what has and has not been shown.

A Minimum Decisive Demonstration shows the smallest necessary chain: problem, permitted evidence, decision, expected outcome, and measurement. The economic test compares a full proposal, a smaller reusable subsystem, the best existing alternative and stopping. Distinguish expense eliminated from funds reallocated.

## Ownership

Identify actual resource, decision, technical and operating owners, and safety/security/privacy approvals when material. Graduation is an accepted operational transfer after repeatable real-world use, not an impressive simulation.
