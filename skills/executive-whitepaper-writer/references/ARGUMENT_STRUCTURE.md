# Argument Structure — Executive Whitepaper Writer

## Objective

Develop an argument that could withstand an executive reader asking "What is true? Why does it matter? What follows? What would change the conclusion?"

Write the paper **after** identifying the logic. Do not publish the construction notes as the final prose.

## The five-line internal argument brief

1. **Decision context:** Why does the reader need this paper now?
2. **Thesis:** What single interpretation or recommendation does the evidence justify?
3. **Supporting chain:** What three to five linked propositions must hold for the thesis to be sound?
4. **Strongest countercase:** What established capability, alternative explanation or trade-off could defeat it?
5. **Action/evidence:** What decision is justified now, or what information must be obtained first?

The supporting chain must be dependent rather than merely topical.

Weak chain:
- Digital products
- AI
- Affiliates
- Governance
- Leadership

Stronger chain:
- A company has launched or is developing several digital commercial capabilities.
- These capabilities create obligations and feedback cycles different from those of its physical products.
- Platform activity metrics may not measure full company economics.
- Independent commercial, financial and operational evidence is therefore relevant to expansion decisions.
- The appropriate organizational form depends on whether current owners can already make and enforce those decisions.

The stronger chain still contains hypotheses. It does not establish a company's operational deficiency.

## Paragraph dependency test

Every paragraph must serve a specific function:
\`context | claim | evidence | mechanism | implication | counterargument | decision\`.

Then test transitions:
- What specific question did the previous paragraph create?
- Does this paragraph answer it or establish the next dependency?
- Does it advance the reader beyond the same thesis repeated in different wording?

Do not force visible linking phrases like "Furthermore" at every transition. Coherence comes from the logic, not connectors.

## Opening test

A valid opening identifies the relevant established situation, the consequential unanswered question and the scope of the inquiry. It does not need a dramatic hook, quotation or new branding.

Do not declare an operating failure without evidence. Distinguish "the company has launched X" from "its infrastructure cannot support X."

## Conclusions

The conclusion should result from the evidence, not be a more emphatic version of the introduction. It should separate:
- what has been established;
- what is the recommended decision or narrow experiment;
- what would prevent or alter implementation.

If the case is still unverified, the correct end may be a bounded investigation rather than reorganization or investment.

## Exhibits and section headings

Use an exhibit only for meaningful comparisons, causal relationships, quantified data or decision alternatives. A decorative capability map is not evidence. Section headings are navigation, not substitutes for prose.

## Structural counterchecks

- Are the body sections ordered by logic, rather than list order or source chronology?
- Does the paper contain a counterargument in proportion to the stakes?
- Did a suggested solution silently become the diagnosed cause?
- Did a source claim about one product become a company-wide conclusion?
- Do paragraphs explain why something matters, rather than only describing what exists?
- Does the author sound neutral without pretending inference is fact?
