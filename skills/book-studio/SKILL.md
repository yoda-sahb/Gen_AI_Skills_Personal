# Book Studio

Build an original book around one author's ideas. Understand the reasoning in its original setting, reorganize it around questions that matter to the reader, and let readers discover the ideas through narrative.

## Establish the book and task

Resolve the active book from the request and its brief or portfolio entry. Read only its relevant sources and decisions. Keep each book's evidence, editorial choices, and versions in that book's records. For a fresh start, use independently verified source material for the new plan and prose. Keep prior drafts in the archive. Match the work to the requested stage. Carry out narrow revisions directly and preserve each claim's certainty, scope, and conditions.

## Reconstruct the author

Identify the main work and edition. Read the relevant passages and select other works by the same author where they clarify a question, development, or exception. Record access separately from actual reading. Map the author's central problem, reasoning, key distinctions, objections, and qualifications to exact locators. Identify which claims can support the requested work. When the needed text is unavailable, report the gap, request the passages, and offer a provisional research plan.

## Reorganize around themes

Group verified claims by the questions they answer, joining passages across source sections or works when they illuminate the same problem. Keep the source-to-theme trail. Whenever you restate a rule in the outline or prose, carry its triggering conditions and material exceptions with it. Order the themes by what the reader must discover to understand the next idea. For each chapter, specify its new insight, source anchors, narrative engine, strongest complication, and consequence. Treat the new arrangement as an editorial choice. Use another thinker only for a named explanatory gap and identify the contribution separately.

## Calibrate the voice before drafting

Treat a user-supplied prose exemplar or an explicitly accepted chapter as the controlling stylistic baseline for that book. Read enough of the opening, a typical middle chapter, and a conclusion to identify its sentence rhythm, explanatory distance, structure, diction, use of examples, and density of source apparatus. Record those traits in the book brief. A request for “Gladwell-style” means accessible narrative discovery, **not** permission to substitute dramatic reportage, literary imitation, or a stock magazine-feature voice for the accepted sample.

Unless a book brief directs otherwise, use calm, readable, reflective narrative nonfiction: ordinary recognizable situations; a genuine question arising from them; plain-language explanation of the author's reasoning; connections to family, worship, work, money, and community where supported; and a clear, proportionate conclusion. Make the reader feel the difficulty before presenting the terminology. Keep chapter openings, transitions, and lengths close to the accepted exemplar without copying its sentences. See [voice calibration](references/voice-calibration.md).

## Turn themes into narrative

Begin with the kind of entry point the accepted voice uses. For reflective prose, an ordinary hypothetical, an apparently minor contradiction, or a recognizable choice may work better than a dramatic historic scene or a startling statistic. Let the reader understand why a reasonable person would choose each side. Introduce the author's insight as the explanation of a problem already made clear, then deepen it through a second example, a qualification, or an overlooked consequence. Return to the reader's life without turning the chapter into a checklist.

Use sustained, connected paragraphs and natural transitions. Explain technical terms only when they become necessary. Integrate essential qualifications in the prose while keeping edition details, methodological cautions, and detailed citations in a separate source guide or notes where possible; never obscure a material condition merely to make the story smoother. Vary chapter mechanisms rather than forcing every chapter through the same puzzle-and-reveal formula.

Document historical particulars and label hypothetical or composite material. Do not invent scenes, quotations, thoughts, or dialogue about historical people. Attribute the source author's reasoning, editorial applications, and outside evidence distinctly. Verify quotations, translations, and consequential claims. If a draft's voice is rejected, diagnose the concrete mismatch against the supplied exemplar and rewrite the affected prose, not merely its introduction.

## Continue or hand off

Use completed work and honor the book's approval requirements. Move from supported architecture to a representative pilot, developed chapters, skeptical review, and release as authorized. Preserve the accepted baseline and record material changes, unresolved evidence, and the next step.
