# Voice calibration and reader experience

## Authority of a writing sample

A user-supplied reference manuscript or explicitly accepted chapter is the **controlling style sample for that book**. It takes precedence over broad descriptors such as “literary,” “accessible,” “popular nonfiction,” or “Malcolm Gladwell-style.” Those descriptors can inform craft but cannot override the observed voice. Never copy the exemplar's distinctive sentences, metaphors, or passages; reproduce its general editorial characteristics in original prose.

Read enough to see the **pattern across the book**, not only the first page: one substantial opening, one ordinary middle chapter, and a conclusion. Record in the book brief the chosen baseline and its observable traits. When no exemplar exists, use the reflective, accessible default below unless the audience or subject requires a different register.

Do not embed or republish a user-provided reference book in the reusable skill. Describe the voice rather than storing its text.

## Default for reflective thematic adaptations

The default reader experience is that of an intelligent general reader being invited to recognize a problem in everyday moral life.

- **Opening:** Start with an ordinary, recognizable situation, a comparison, or an imagined decision. “Imagine” and “suppose” are useful when the scene is hypothetical. The people need not be extraordinary, and the action need not be dramatic. Establish what makes both sides understandable before identifying the tension.
- **Question:** Let the example raise one specific question. Resist announcing the full thesis before the reader sees why it is needed. Do not manufacture suspense, force a twist, or place an impressive statistic at the beginning solely to create a hook.
- **Explanation:** Introduce the source thinker once the reader knows the difficulty. Explain the idea in direct, fluent English. Translate or gloss a technical term when it earns its place; avoid piling up labels, Arabic vocabulary, or abstract frameworks before a human example.
- **Development:** Connect the source idea to a second ordinary situation, a complication, a counterexample, or the reader's own conduct. Acknowledge circumstances in which the initial conclusion might change. Let the reasoning unfold in successive full paragraphs rather than stage directions or a sequence of isolated punchlines.
- **Ending:** Conclude with a meaningful change in how the reader sees the problem, a practical distinction, or a question that remains worth carrying forward. Avoid generic inspiration, obvious moralizing, and repeated aphorisms.

The narrator may use “we,” “you,” and direct questions when natural, but should not lecture, flatter, or presume that every reader shares the same situation. Keep the tone calm, perceptive, and conversational without becoming casual, sensational, or psychologically overconfident.

## Rhythm, architecture, and exposition

Prefer coherent paragraphs with a clear causal flow. Vary sentence length for clarity, not theatrical impact. Use short sentences when an idea needs emphasis; do not turn every paragraph into a one-sentence revelation. Favor precise ordinary words over inflated phrases such as “the extraordinary paradox,” “a shocking truth,” or “what nobody realizes.”

Let chapters be as short as the idea deserves. For a compact thematic adaptation, several relatively short chapters grouped into larger movements may fit the reference better than a few long investigative essays. A longer first chapter may establish the central problem; later chapters can extend it through distinct tensions. Do not impose a fixed word count without a brief or sample supporting it. Use chapter and subheading frequency consistent with the accepted book; avoid excessive labels such as “The Framework,” “Practical Takeaways,” or “The Counterintuitive Reveal” when the reference reads as continuous prose.

Prose should explain a complete thought before moving on. A chapter should be satisfying as a piece of reading, not merely an argument map expanded into paragraphs. At the same time, avoid recycling the same basic example or conclusion under new chapter titles.

## Source fidelity without breaking the narrative

Keep three layers distinct even when the main prose flows smoothly:

1. **What the author actually argues**, supported by recoverable passages, with its conditions and exceptions.
2. **How the editor connects passages**, explicitly identifiable as reconstruction.
3. **How a contemporary example applies the idea**, clearly an illustration rather than a recorded historical episode or the author's own example.

The reader need not encounter citation machinery in every paragraph. Integrate attribution naturally and place detailed editions, page locators, verification status, and editorial disclosures in notes or a source guide when the book's format allows. But do not hide essential limitations in the notes: if a principle applies only to certain cases, that condition belongs in the narrative.

Do not invent historical dialogue, private motives, experiments, field research, statistics, or named episodes. Clearly label imagined scenarios. If the main argument depends on the author's actual example, use the documented example and keep its verifiable details accurate.

## Calibration and revision test

Before extensive drafting, compare a representative passage with the accepted sample on these questions:

1. **Entry point:** Would the reference begin with this kind of example, or does the draft sound like a reported feature when the reference is reflective?
2. **Sequence:** Does the reader encounter a recognizable difficulty before a cluster of concepts or biographical facts?
3. **Cadence:** Do paragraphs connect logically at a comparable pace, or are they broken into rhetorical fragments and formulaic reveals?
4. **Accessibility:** Could a reader unfamiliar with the field follow the reasoning without an explanatory sidebar?
5. **Attribution:** Can the reader distinguish sourced teaching from modern application without being distracted by constant procedural caveats?
6. **Ending:** Does the chapter deepen understanding rather than conclude with an unearned slogan or a productivity exercise?
7. **Continuity:** Does it sound like the other approved chapters of **this** book, rather than like a generic version of an admired popular writer?

If a user says the writing does not match the sample, inspect and name the concrete differences, then rewrite enough of the **actual chapter** to correct them. Do not treat changing the title, adding a hypothetical opening, or adjusting typography as a successful voice revision. Preserve the accepted baseline in the book's records and check later chapters against it.
