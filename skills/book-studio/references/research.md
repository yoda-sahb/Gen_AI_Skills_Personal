# Research

## Scope the corpus

Define the question and evidence needed for the requested output. Prioritize essential sources, useful context, and optional leads. Do not expand the corpus indefinitely. Establish which sources and passages must be read before the proposed argument can be supported.

Identify the primary work and edition, then select works by the same author that clarify development, method, qualifications, or apparent contradictions. Recover the original problem and technical terms in context. If two passages appear to conflict, examine the conditions or period of each and record any disagreement that remains.

Maintain a source-status table:

| Source and edition | Access or verified location | Reading actually completed | Intended role | Unresolved issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Separate access from reading: accessible full text can still be unread. Use precise statuses such as selected passages read, complete text read, translation read, contents only, secondary report, or inaccessible. Record passage ranges and dates where needed. Never upgrade a status from a search result, AI summary, or previous memo alone.

## Map the argument

Reconstruct the central question, intellectual context, reasoning, definitions, evidence, competing views, qualifications, and what the source does not establish. Follow the author's argument before designing a contemporary application.

Use recoverable claim records:

| Claim ID | Claim and attribution type | Source, edition, locator | Supporting passage or faithful paraphrase | Qualification or objection | Verification status | Chapter use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Distinguish primary-author views, other scholars' views, historical or empirical findings, editorial inference, and hypothetical illustration. Keep interpretations with their reasons and labels rather than presenting them as direct quotations or source categories.

Verify quotations against the text. Identify translators; distinguish published translations from new translations. Preserve printed versus digital pagination and volume numbers. Use section, paragraph, timestamp, or stable passage identifiers when pages are unavailable. Check scans when OCR could alter a consequential claim. Treat citations found inside a source as unverified leads until independently checked when the argument depends on them.

## Integrate selectively

For every substantial supporting contributor, name the gap left by the anchor author and explain what the contributor adds. Identify the contribution’s domain and limits. Distinguish ethical, historical, empirical, therapeutic, and normative authority.

Finish with an argument map supported to the depth needed for the next stage, its strongest objections, and explicit gaps. Do not claim a complete corpus audit or comprehensive intellectual history from partial reading. When access prevents a supported argument, produce a provisional plan and source request.
